## intro
- minutes: "about 25 minutes"
- why: "A reviewer that read the wrong file sounds as confident as one that read the right one. Picking by majority or by tone puts a wrong room on the library's website."
- can: "weigh disagreeing reviews by what each one read and how recent its evidence is"
- can: "settle what the files can settle and ask the one question that settles what they can't"
- can: "write the decision down with its evidence"
- payoff: "weigh disagreeing reviews by what each one read and how recent its evidence is"
- read: "[Best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices#know-what-subagents-cost): what a fresh reviewer gives you, and what it doesn't."

## warm-up

### R1 | Warm-up | What the capstone's check read
- tier: challenge
- skill: check
- from: b-capstone
- beat: ask
- ask: The capstone's check passed on your March guide. Priya asks whether that means it's short and kind enough to print. What do you tell her?
- wrong: Yes: a pass means the whole guide is ready
- because: "The check reads your two files, so it can't tell how you reached them, and it doesn't measure length or tone."
- right: No: the check reads facts in two files, not length or tone
- wrong: Yes, because Claude wrote the draft and the check agreed
- because: "Two routes both work: brief Claude well, then check its draft; or ask for a quick draft, then check it hard. What doesn't work is skipping the check."
- reveal: "What only you can judge: whether the guide fits on half a page, whether the access line is kind as well as accurate, and which one line changes if Priya confirms the lift on Friday."

### R2 | Warm-up | A tiny change on every page
- tier: challenge
- skill: pick-pattern
- from: a-2
- beat: ask
- ask: Maya wants the word "Questions" changed to "Contact" on all three session pages. How do you run it?
- wrong: One subagent per page, then check what each one changed
- because: "Small, mechanical and the same everywhere: one session is cheapest and easiest to check."
- wrong: Three parallel sessions, each in its own worktree
- because: "Refuse: it pays only when sessions would edit the same files at once, and nothing collides here"
- right: One session: one request, then read the three pages
- reveal: "Small, mechanical and the same everywhere: one session is cheapest and easiest to check."

## steps

### W1 | Worked example step 1 of 4 | List what each reviewer read
- tier: hinted
- skill: review-diff
- where: editor
- beat: predict
- ask: Two reviewers flag the June page, one says it's clear. How do you decide which findings to act on?
- option: Act on any finding: two flags out of three means the page has problems
- because: "Counting flags treats every reviewer as equally informed. A reviewer that read last March's notes can flag a June page for the wrong reason."
- option: Ask Claude to review the page again and go with its answer
- because: "A fourth opinion is another vote. If it reads the same files as the others, it inherits their blind spots."
- right: Check what each reviewer read, whether anything is newer, and the line it used
- reveal: "A finding is only as good as its sources. Scope and date decide most disagreements."
- beat: do
- verify: self
- done-label: I read the three Read lines
- goal: "Open the three reports in the files tab, in `fixit/summer/reviews/`."
- note: "Accuracy read `june.md`, the June booking signed on 19 May and the May meeting notes. It didn't read the late email of 10 June."
- note: "Accessibility read the three session pages and last March's notes, `march/meeting-notes-2027-02-20.md`. It didn't read the June booking, the May notes or the late email."
- note: "Privacy read all four public pages. It needs no sources: it checks for personal numbers."
- note: "The late email arrived at 16:40 yesterday, after the reviews were set up. Two reviewers never saw it."
- hint: "Each report starts with a **Read:** line."

### Y2 | Your turn step 2 of 4 | Decide what the files can't settle
- tier: hinted
- skill: reconcile
- where: claude
- beat: ask
- ask: Nobody's evidence covers "Free parking at the centre." What do you do before Maya publishes the August page?
- wrong: Keep it: Sam knows the centre, and no reviewer objected to it
- because: "No reviewer objected because none of them had a source for it. Silence isn't confirmation."
- wrong: Ask Claude to find out whether Northside has free parking
- because: "Claude can only read what's in the folder, and the bookings connection, which doesn't cover parking. It would be guessing."
- right: Take the line out for now, and ask Kofi about the car park
- wrong: Hold the whole August page until Kofi answers
- because: "Holding the whole page is safe, but the rest of it is confirmed, and people booking for August need it today."
- reveal: "One question to the person who knows, and no unconfirmed claim on the page meanwhile. Add it back when Kofi answers."
- hint: "What the files can't settle is a question for a person, not a judgment for Claude."
