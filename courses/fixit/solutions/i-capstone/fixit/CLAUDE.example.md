# Millbrook Fixers: working on event material

Public pages are read by members of the public, some with access needs, and printed by the venue.

## Which source wins
When sources disagree, use the higher one and say which you used:
1. The venue's written confirmation: a signed booking, or an email from the venue's staff confirming the booking. If there are several, the newest wins on what it covers.
2. Our latest meeting notes, or the organizer's notes for that session.
3. `sources/group-notes.md`.
4. Old flyers, newsletters and forwarded listings. Never use them for times, capacity, access, parking or contacts.

## Facts we can't confirm
If a source says something needs confirming, or two sources disagree and neither is the venue's written confirmation, publish it as **being confirmed** and give the venue's public number. Never present it as fact, and never leave it out silently.

## Contacts
Only the venue's public number goes on a public page. Treat every other phone number and email address as private, wherever you find it: notes, rosters, flyers.

## House style
Plain English, short lines. Times as 10:30am.
